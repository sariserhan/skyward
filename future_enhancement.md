## Future Enhancements

### 1. Deboarding / Arrival Simulation

After landing, simulate:

* passenger deboarding order
* aisle congestion
* tight connections
* transfer passengers
* airport exit flow
* wheelchair / assistance passengers

### 2. Baggage System

Add:

* checked baggage
* overhead-bin usage
* bags loaded onto the wrong flight
* delayed luggage
* missing luggage
* baggage claim
* transfer-bag handling

### 3. Immigration / Customs / Connections

For international flights:

* passport control
* customs
* connecting flights
* missed connections
* security re-screening
* terminal transfers

### 4. Airport Operations / Irregularities

Later scenarios could include:

* wrong boarding pass
* passenger at wrong gate
* duplicate seat assignment
* unattended bag
* prohibited-item alerts
* document problems
* gate changes
* delayed passengers
* aircraft changes

Security gameplay should focus on observable events, documents, baggage, and behavior—not demographic profiling.

### 5. Real Airports and Routes

Once the game has traction:

* recreate licensed or officially partnered airport layouts
* use real routes
* use real terminals and gates where available
* reproduce recognizable airport operations

Example:

**Washington Dulles → Istanbul**

A player could operate a real-world-style international route rather than a fictional one.

### 6. Airline / Airport / Manufacturer Partnerships

Potential commercial partnerships:

* airlines
* airports
* aircraft manufacturers
* tourism boards

Instead of banner advertising, use branded playable content.

Example:

**Official Turkish Airlines Istanbul Challenge**

or:

**Washington Dulles Boarding Challenge**

Possible sponsorship could include:

* airline livery
* branded aircraft
* official route
* airport environment
* leaderboard event
* destination promotion

### 7. Real Trip Mode — Major Long-Term Feature

A passenger waiting for a real flight could scan their boarding pass inside the game.

The game identifies available trip information such as:

**IAD → IST**
**Airline: Turkish Airlines**
**Flight: TK***
**Seat: 21A**
**Departure: Today**

Then it creates a game based on the trip the player is actually about to take.

The player could see:

> **You are actually flying this route today. Can you board your aircraft faster than the simulation?**

Depending on available data, the scenario could incorporate:

* origin airport
* destination airport
* airline
* flight number
* departure time
* seat
* cabin
* aircraft type
* terminal
* gate

Some information may come directly from the boarding pass; other information could later come from airline/airport/flight-data integrations.

### 8. Airport Waiting Experience

This could eventually turn waiting at the gate into part of the game.

For example:

> **Your flight boards in 34 minutes.**

**Play Your Flight**

The passenger scans the ticket and gets a personalized challenge.

Their actual seat could even appear in the simulation:

**You: Seat 21A**

They could watch their simulated passenger board along with everyone else.

That makes the game personally relevant instead of being just another airport simulator.

### 9. Real Flight Social Challenge

An even later version could allow passengers on the same real flight to participate in the same challenge.

Example:

**TK008 — IAD → IST**

47 actual passengers on that flight are playing.

Everyone receives the same passenger manifest simulation and tries to find the fastest boarding strategy.

Leaderboard:

1. Passenger 14 — 12:41
2. Passenger 37 — 12:53
3. Passenger 08 — 13:02

That could give the product a very unusual real-world viral loop.

---

## Product Rule

Do **not** build these features into V1.

V1 stays focused on one question:

> **Can optimizing the boarding of autonomous passengers be genuinely fun and addictive?**

If that succeeds, the game can gradually evolve from a small boarding simulator into a much larger **real-world passenger journey and airport operations game**.
